# Phase B Results — Replicating the BTC→Altcoin Literature on Our Own Data

Generated by `research/btc_effects.py` → `logs/btc_research.json`.

**Sample:** Binance spot, 10 USDT pairs, **2024-07-30 → 2026-07-30**
(4,378 aligned 4h candles; 8,758 1h candles). Bootstrap CIs from 1,000
resamples. All windows causal — volatility/regime classifiers are shifted so no
candle helps classify itself.

**Headline:** four of the six analyses **disagree** with the published
literature. That is the expected consequence of testing 2013–2019-era findings
on 2024–2026 data, and the disagreements are reported as findings, not errors.

---

## A methodology correction worth recording

The regime-propagation analysis first used the *unconditional* probability that
BTC and an alt share a regime as its baseline. That is a different quantity
from what the treatment measures: the follow-rate is computed only on rows
where the regimes currently **disagree**, while the unconditional rate is
dominated by rows where they already agree.

With that mismatched baseline the result was **−0.222 mean lift, 0/9 coins
positive** — i.e. "BTC flips actually predict *divergence*". After replacing it
with a matched control (identical convergence question, evaluated on non-flip
rows, restricted identically to disagreeing rows) the result inverted to
**+0.133 mean lift, 9/9 coins positive, 8/9 significant**.

The wrong baseline would have shipped the opposite conclusion to users. Both
numbers are in git history; the corrected one is what the site will show.

## Q1 — Correlation structure ✅ CONFIRMS Aslanidis et al. (2019)

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Mean full-sample BTC–alt correlation | **0.732** |
| All correlations positive | yes (9/9) |
| Mean 90d rolling range (max − min) | **0.320** |

Highest coupling ETH **0.819**, lowest XRP **0.655**. Every 95% bootstrap CI
excludes zero. The 0.32 average swing in the 90-day rolling correlation
confirms the "positive but time-varying" finding directly — a single static
correlation number would misrepresent the relationship.

## Q2 — Lead–lag ⚠️ PARTIALLY confirms Sifat et al. (2019)

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Mean **contemporaneous** correlation | **0.772** |
| Mean 1h-lagged correlation (BTC leads) | **≈ 0.006** |
| Granger: BTC→alt significant | 6 / 9 |
| Granger: alt→BTC significant | 4 / 9 |
| Bi-directional | 4 / 9 |

Sifat's bi-directionality is partly reproduced (4/9 pairs, and ETH/SOL show no
significant causality in *either* direction). But the more consequential
finding is the gap between the two correlation rows: coins move with BTC
**contemporaneously (0.77)** while BTC's predictive correlation one hour ahead
is **essentially zero (~0.006)**.

**This is the single most important result for the trading application.** The
BTC–alt relationship is a *co-movement* phenomenon, not a *lead* phenomenon at
hourly resolution. "BTC just moved, so the alt will move next" is not supported
by our data.

## Q3 — Volatility spillover ❌ CONTRADICTS Koutmos (2018), ✅ SUPPORTS Yi et al. (2018)

VAR(4) forecast-error variance decomposition, horizon 10, on standardized
squared 4h returns:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Mean BTC share of alt volatility variance | **28.4%** |
| Range across coins | 14.0% – 48.4% |
| BTC's own variance that is self-driven | **92.4%** |

Koutmos found BTC "the dominant contributor of return and volatility
spillovers". On our sample BTC explains a **minority** (28%) of altcoin
volatility variance — meaningful, clearly the largest single external source,
but far from dominant. Yi et al.'s more careful "major transmitter but not the
sole one" is the better description of 2024–2026 markets.

BTC being 92.4% self-driven is the mirror image: BTC largely does its own
thing, and pushes about a quarter of that onto everything else.

## Q4 — Crash asymmetry ❌ CONTRADICTS Demir et al. (2021) and Šíla et al. (2024)

BTC 4h shocks beyond ±2 trailing-30d σ: **109 up-shocks, 143 down-shocks**.
Mean alt return over the following 24h:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Mean asymmetry (\|down response\| − \|up response\|) | **−0.22 pp** |
| Coins where down-shocks hit harder | **2 / 9** |

The literature predicts BTC declines hit alts harder than rallies lift them.
**Our sample shows the opposite**, and the pattern is mean-reverting rather
than momentum: after BTC *up*-shocks alts tend to drift down (SOL −0.59%,
ADA −0.64% over 24h), while after BTC *down*-shocks several alts rebound
(XRP +0.89%, ADA +0.18%).

Plausible explanations, none tested here: the 2024–26 era (spot ETFs,
institutional participation, deeper liquidity) differs structurally from the
2015–2019 samples those papers used; and our simplified event study is not the
NARDL/TVP-VAR machinery they employed. **Treat as a genuine sample
disagreement requiring further work, not as a refutation.**

## Q5 — Regime propagation ✅ SUPPORTS the "BTC leads" heuristic (which has *no* academic backing)

When BTC's 24h trend regime flips, does a currently-disagreeing alt converge to
BTC's new regime within 24h?

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Mean follow rate after a BTC flip | **44.3%** |
| Mean matched-control rate | **31.0%** |
| **Mean lift** | **+13.3 pp** |
| Coins with positive lift | **9 / 9** |
| Significant at p<0.05 | **8 / 9** (DOGE p=0.069) |
| Median lag | **8–12 hours** |

Strongest per coin: **BNB +19.2 pp** (p<0.001), **LINK +17.6 pp** (p=0.0001,
fastest at 8h), **SOL +17.0 pp** (p=0.0001). Weakest: DOGE +7.8 pp, the only
non-significant coin — consistent with its more meme-driven, idiosyncratic
behaviour.

Phase A found the "altseason / BTC-leads" framework has **zero peer-reviewed
support**. Our data says the underlying intuition is real *at the regime level*
even though it is absent at the hourly-return level (Q2) — a coherent picture:
BTC does not predict the next hour's alt return, but a BTC *regime change*
raises the odds an alt follows within roughly half a day.

This is the finding that feeds the Phase C propagation forecast.

## Q6 — Beta, calm vs stress ❌ CONTRADICTS the Bouri et al. (2019) prediction

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Mean BTC beta | **1.274** |
| Mean R² | **0.529** |
| Mean (stress beta − calm beta) | **−0.303** |
| Coins with higher beta in stress | **0 / 9** |

Altcoins **amplify** BTC moves (β > 1 for most; ADA 1.45, SOL 1.31). But beta
*falls* in high-volatility regimes for all nine coins, whereas herding theory
predicted it should rise.

Interpretation, stated with appropriate uncertainty: in turbulent periods alts
develop more idiosyncratic variance (their own liquidations, narratives,
unlocks), which lowers the fitted beta even while correlation stays high. Note
this measures **beta**, not correlation, and Bouri's herding tests use a
different statistic (CSAD) — so this is suggestive evidence against, not a
direct refutation.

## What Phase C can and cannot build on

**Solid ground:**
- Per-coin coupling state (Q1) — correlation levels and their percentile
  ranking are stable, well-estimated, and vary meaningfully across coins.
- Regime propagation (Q5) — a real, significant, per-coin effect with a
  measured lag and a proper control. This is the one candidate with genuine
  predictive content.

**Explicitly NOT to build on:**
- Hourly BTC→alt prediction (Q2) — the predictive correlation is ~0.006.
  Anything framed as "BTC moved, expect the alt to follow this hour" would be
  unsupported by our own measurements.
- Crash asymmetry (Q4) — our sample contradicts the literature; too unstable
  to expose as a user-facing signal.

**Mandatory before Q5 ships to users:** everything above is *in-sample* over
2024-07→2026-07. The propagation effect must be re-measured walk-forward on
data after its estimation window before it appears on the site with a
probability attached, exactly as the plan's validation gate requires.
